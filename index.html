S2M-Trek: From Single to Multi-Sphere Transport via Per-Frame Deep Sets on a Wheel-Legged Robot

Zong Chen1,†, Xuebin Li2, Jinpeng Xiao1, Shaoyang Li1, Ben Liu1, Min Li1, Zhouping Yin1, Yiqun Li1,*

1School of Mechanical Science and Engineering, Huazhong University of Science and Technology
2School of Mathematics, Harbin Institute of Technology
†skelon_chan@hust.edu.cn  ·  *liyiqun@hust.edu.cn Corresponding author

A single PFDS policy scales dynamic loco-manipulation from one to five free-rolling spheres on the unfenced back plate of the S3 wheel-legged quadruped — no grippers, no fences, no mechanical stops.

Abstract

Conventional object transport typically relies on containers, fences, or tethers to secure the payload. Here, a wheel-legged robot transports up to five identical, free-rolling steel spheres on an open back plate without physical restraints. Because the spheres are indistinguishable, their observation-slot assignments may change independently across history frames, creating a per-frame permutation symmetry that standard history-concatenation set encoders do not enforce. This mismatch becomes a bottleneck beyond two spheres: under the same PPO budget, flat and branch-wise MLPs plateau at or below two spheres, while a history-concatenation Deep Sets baseline (DSHC) also stalls unless slot assignments are randomized during training. We introduce Per-Frame Deep Sets (PFDS), which pools objects invariantly within each frame before temporal fusion. PFDS is Gframe-invariant by construction, and we prove universal approximation of continuous Gframe-invariant policies. In a 2×2 ablation over architecture and slot randomization, PFDS reaches five spheres across all five seeds and achieves 100% no-drop transport, whereas DSHC relies on augmentation for reliable curriculum progression. We also distill the privileged PFDS teacher into TactSet, which observes only a Gframe-invariant binary union contact map and retains 75% no-drop performance with five spheres. Finally, we run the S2M-Trek deployment stack closed-loop on a physical wheel-legged robot for every sphere count k=1,…,5. The measured tactile maps contain one connected component per sphere but no persistent identity, complementing the quantitative simulation results.

Main Contributions

  1. A symmetry-mismatch diagnosis. We formalize multi-sphere transport as a Gframe-invariant policy learning problem and derive the quotient ratio (N!)H−1— about 1.7×106 for N=5 spheres and H=4 history frames — as a quantitative measure of the non-physical redundancy that standard history-concatenation encoders fail to collapse.
  2. The PFDS encoder. A minimal modification of Deep Sets that pools within each history frame before temporal readout, achieving intrinsic per-frame permutation invariance. We back this with formal proofs of Gframe-invariance and universal approximation.
  3. Architecture vs. augmentation, untangled. An eight-encoder comparison plus a 2×2 ablation (encoder × slot randomization) cleanly separates the architectural pathway from the data-augmentation pathway to multi-sphere robustness. Five-seed runs confirm PFDS reliability.
  4. TactSet and physical validation. We distill the privileged PFDS teacher into a student using a 16×16 binary union tactile map. TactSet retains 75% no-drop transport with five spheres under the original simulator assets and 85% in the matched evaluation harness. We also run the same deployment stack closed-loop on the physical robot for k=1,…,5; its tactile maps show one connected component per sphere without persistent identity.

Our Method in a Nutshell

The problem. When a quadruped carries multiple identical free-rolling spheres, the observation tensor X ∈ ℝH×N×d indexes balls by an arbitrary slot id at each of H history frames. Because the balls are identical, swapping two slot assignments in any single frame produces a physically equivalent state — so the correct symmetry group is the per-frame product Gframe = (SN)H. Standard Deep Sets applied to history-concatenated tokens only respect the much smaller diagonal subgroup Gdiag, leaving (N!)H−1-fold non-physical variation that the policy must learn to ignore from scratch.

PFDS (Per-Frame Deep Sets). We change where the pooling happens, not how big the network is. A shared MLP φ embeds each per-ball feature, masked sum pools within each frame t to a frame embedding ht, and a temporal MLP ρ reads out [h1; …; hH]. This single re-ordering of operations makes the encoder intrinsically invariant to independent per-frame slot permutations, collapsing the entire Gframe orbit to one representation.

Training & distillation. The PFDS teacher is trained in Isaac Lab with PPO under a multi-criterion curriculum that gradually increases the sphere count from 1 to 5. We then distill the teacher into TactSet via DAgger, replacing the privileged ball state with a 16×16 Boolean union contact map — the same physical contacts visible to a real tactile array. Because the Boolean union is symmetric in its arguments, the tactile observation is Gframe-invariant by construction, semantically aligning the student input with the teacher's quotient representation.

Per-Frame Deep Sets (PFDS) Encoder

The core idea. Per-object observations xt,i are pooled independently within each history frame t to produce frame embeddings ht; these are then concatenated and processed by the temporal readout MLP ρ. Because pooling occurs within each frame, the encoder is intrinsically invariant to independent per-frame permutations (Gframe-invariant) — collapsing the (N!)H−1 non-physical redundancy left by standard history-concatenation encoders.

Beyond the Training Range: Zero-Shot k = 6, 7, 8

The curriculum stops at five spheres, but the policies also extrapolate zero-shot to six, seven, and eight. The PFDS teacher retains every sphere in 98% / 95% / 93% of episodes, while the TactSet student reaches 80% / 59% / 33% full-retention rates, respectively.

The table also reports strict success, which adds motion and support-margin requirements to full-horizon payload retention. The widening gap at larger counts shows that retaining every sphere and maintaining the stricter transport criteria measure different aspects of performance.

Results

A summary of the four headline experiments. Full numbers, ablations, and confidence intervals are in the paper.

Curriculum reach
PFDS reaches five spheres with and without slot-permutation augmentation across all five seeds. DSHC reaches five spheres with augmentation but stalls at two without it; Flat-MLP and Branch-MLP plateau at or below two spheres.
Advancement criteria
The six promotion criteria combine episode length, support margin, unsafe-state and edge fractions, and velocity and yaw tracking errors. Promotion requires all six gates to be met together.
Seed reliability
All five independent PFDS seeds reach five spheres, supporting the repeatability of curriculum progression across random seeds.
TactSet distillation
The DAgger-distilled student reaches 75% no-drop on five spheres under the original simulator assets and 85% in the matched evaluation harness, using only a 16×16 binary tactile map.
